How prayer times are calculated

Muslim prayer times are defined by the position of the sun, so they can be computed astronomically for any place and date. SalahTime uses the standard solar-declination and equation-of-time formulation: Fajr and Isha are set when the sun reaches a fixed angle below the horizon (twilight), Sunrise and Sunset at 0.833° below the horizon (accounting for refraction), Dhuhr at solar noon, and Asr when an object's shadow reaches a set multiple of its length. Maghrib is at sunset.

Different religious authorities use slightly different twilight angles and precautionary minute offsets — that is why prayer times differ between methods. SalahTime supports the eleven official methods below and automatically selects the right default for each city (for example, the Egyptian General Authority of Survey for Egypt). You can switch methods any time on the home page.

Asr: Standard vs Hanafi

Asr begins when the shadow of an object reaches a certain length beyond its noon shadow. The Standard setting (Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali schools) starts Asr when the shadow equals the object's height; the Hanafi setting starts it when the shadow reaches twice the object's height, giving a later Asr. Choose the one that matches your school of thought.

High-latitude adjustment

In far northern or southern locations the sun may barely set in summer or barely rise in winter, making twilight-angle times extreme or undefined. SalahTime applies an angle-based adjustment: Fajr and Isha are limited to a proportion of the night (angle ÷ 60 × night length), the same approach used by major prayer-time APIs.

Imsak

Imsak — the time to stop eating before fasting — is shown as 10 minutes before Fajr for all methods.
